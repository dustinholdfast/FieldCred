How to verify an OSHA 10 card

The card is a student course completion card, not a license. OSHA will not look it up for you. Here is what actually works.

People say “OSHA 10 certification.” OSHA does not. The plastic or paper wallet card from a 10-hour or 30-hour class is a student course completion card. It is not a certification and it is not a license. Treating it like a license is how crews get surprised at the gate.

Source for every fact below: OSHA Outreach Training Program FAQs. If a vendor’s page disagrees with OSHA, the vendor is wrong.

What the card is — and is not

  • Proof that a student finished an OSHA Outreach 10- or 30-hour class (Construction, General Industry, or Maritime) — or the 15-hour Disaster Site Worker class.
  • Not a federal requirement. Outreach is voluntary. OSHA does not require 10- or 30-hour training. A state, employer, union, or GC may still require it on a site.
  • Not a substitute for OSHA standard-specific training. Hazard training required by a standard still belongs to the employer.
  • Issued to the student within 90 days of class. If it has not shown up, the student contacts the trainer — then outreach@dol.gov if the trainer does not produce it.

OSHA will not verify the card for you

For privacy reasons, OSHA does not provide individual verification of student course completion cards or authorized trainer cards. Verification of Outreach card authenticity is at the discretion of the cardholder.

There is no national database. OSHA does not operate, maintain, or acknowledge websites that claim to look up every OSHA 10/30 card by serial number. If a site sells you “instant OSHA verification,” it is not OSHA.

Do not look for holograms, DOL watermarks, or a federal serial portal. Those are folklore. They are not how OSHA tells you to verify a card, and they are easy to fake. Use the two methods OSHA actually publishes.

Two ways that count

1. The authorized trainer (paper or plastic)

The cardholder gives you the name and contact of the authorized trainer who issued the card. Every authorized Outreach trainer must keep class records for five years. Call or email that trainer and confirm the student, the class type (10 or 30, Construction / General Industry / Maritime), and the class date.

If the class is older than five years, the trainer is no longer required to keep the file. That is not proof the card is fake — it is a records gap. At that point you are into jobsite recency policy, not OSHA lookup.

2. The QR code on the back of a plastic card

On a plastic Outreach student or trainer card, scan the QR code on the back. It does not dump the record into a public national registry. It gives you contact information for the OTI Education Center that processed the card. That center can verify it.

Paper cards and many online-class cards do not have that QR. Then you are back to method 1.

Confirm the trainer before anyone sits in class

Authorized Outreach trainers are not OSHA employees. Before you buy a class, ask to see the trainer’s current Authorized Trainer card. Front of the card: trainer name and authorization expiration. It also lists the Authorizing Training Organization (usually an OTI Education Center). Call that ATO if the card looks off. Trainers must show proof of current authorization on request.

Expiration: federal vs the jobsite

Student course completion cards for Construction, General Industry, and Disaster Site Worker have no federal expiration. Maritime student-card expiration ended 1 April 2019. OSHA does not make the holder recertify on a federal clock.

Jobsites still set recency. A GC that wants an OSHA 30 taken in the last five years is allowed to want that. Track your recency date in the roster even when OSHA does not print an expiry on the card.

Replacement cards

Only the original trainer can replace a card, and only if the class was within five years. One replacement per student per class. OSHA cannot replace a card. OSHA does not keep student records for Outreach classes delivered by trainers.

What FieldCred does with an OSHA card

FieldCred does not have an OSHA API. Nobody does — OSHA does not offer one. We store the upload (photo or PDF), the card number you type, and — if an admin actually looks it up — a timestamped attestation: “I checked this on the portal above and confirmed it’s real.” That stamps the admin’s email and time. It is a human record, not a federal check.
