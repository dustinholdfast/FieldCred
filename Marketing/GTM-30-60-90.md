## 0. Read this part first

### The plan is time-constrained, not idea-constrained

5–10 hrs/week × 13 weeks = **65–130 hours total**. That is the entire budget for engineering, selling, onboarding, and support. Everything below is sequenced against that number, not against what an ideal GTM plan looks like.

Where those hours go:

| Bucket | Hours | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering to unblock billing | 18–24 | Non-negotiable. Detailed in §4. |
| Prospecting + outbound | 30–50 | The only thing that produces customers |
| Demos (16–20 × 45 min incl. prep) | 14–18 | |
| Pilot onboarding + check-ins (6–8 pilots × 3 hrs) | 18–24 | White-glove. Do not skip. |
| Conversion conversations | 5–8 | |
| **Total** | **85–124** | Fits 10 hrs/wk. Does **not** fit 5 hrs/wk. |

**Honest read:** at 5 hrs/week this plan lands 1–2 paying customers by day 90. At 10 hrs/week it lands 3–5. If you can only do 5, cut the engineering scope (§4 "minimum viable money path" only) and cut cohort 2 entirely.

### The funnel math that produces 3–5 paying

Work backwards:

```
3–5 paying customers at day 90
  ← 8–10 pilots started, converting at ~45%
      (45% is realistic for a free pilot WITH a signed agreement and a
       loaded roster; it's ~15% without both)
  ← 16–20 qualified demos, ~50% convert to pilot
  ← 350–500 targeted outbound touches at ~4–5% touch-to-meeting
  ← a target list of ~200 named companies, worked 2–3 touches deep
```

**Every pilot must start by day 50** for a 30-day pilot + a 2-week conversion window to fit inside 90 days. That's the hard deadline in this plan. Pilots started after day 50 are cohort 2 and convert around day 110–120.

### Three things I'd push back on before you start

**1. Kill the content plan for now.** `Marketing/content-strategy.md` prescribes 1 SEO article/week + biweekly newsletter + 3–4 LinkedIn posts/week. That's 6–10 hrs/week by itself — your entire budget — and SEO pays out in month 6–12, not month 3. You asked for revenue in 90 days. **Cut to one LinkedIn post per week, repurposed from a pilot conversation, and zero articles until you have a paying customer.** Revisit content at day 90 when you know which pillar actually landed.

**2. Your pricing is too low, and I have numbers.** Comparable flat-rate safety platforms start at **$349/month** for a 10-person operation. Per-user safety software runs **$5–15/user/mo basic, $15–50 premium** — a 100-worker contractor on the cheap end of per-user is already at $500/mo. And here's the one that matters: contractors on prequalification networks pay **ISNetworld $875–$3,000/year** and **Avetta $450–$900/year**, often both — **$4,000–$8,000/year just to stay listed.** Your Starter at $99/mo ($1,188/yr) is priced below what they already spend to *claim* they're compliant. **Recommendation: list Starter at $199 and Growth at $499. Offer $99/$349 as a locked founding-customer rate.** You keep your current numbers as the discount, and you stop signalling "toy."

**3. Do not build self-serve signup in the first 90 days.** You have a manual `tenants.php` FTP step and no signup automation. At 8 pilots that's ~40 minutes of founder time each — annoying but cheap. Building self-serve costs 20+ hours you don't have and removes the single best pilot-survival mechanic: **you loading their roster yourself.** Pilots die because nobody enters data. Do it for them. Manual onboarding is a feature until customer #15.

## 1. Narrow the target. This is the highest-leverage decision.

`Marketing/audience-icp.md` names three personas and says marketing must work for all three. At 5–10 hrs/week, cold, that is fatal. **Pick one.**

### Recommended segment: specialty trade subcontractors, 30–150 field workers, working on industrial/energy/utility sites

Why this one and not the others:

- **The gate is literally enforced.** Refinery, plant, utility, and large industrial sites have real gate access control, site-specific orientations, and contractor management requirements. Your product's central claim is a gate moment. Sell where the gate exists.
- **They already pay for compliance.** Anyone in ISNetworld / Avetta / Veriforce has a proven line item for "proving we're compliant." Budget objection is pre-answered.
- **They're findable.** ISNetworld alone lists 70,000+ contractors. GC subcontractor prequalification lists, ABC/AGC chapter member directories, and state contractor license databases are public and filterable.
- **The credential density is highest.** OSHA 10/30, TWIC, confined space, fall protection, hot work, NCCER, operator certs, site orientations — 6–12 credentials per worker instead of 2. Your spreadsheet-failure story is most acute here.

Trades to target first: **electrical, mechanical/HVAC, industrial insulation & scaffolding, welding/pipefitting, crane & rigging.**

### Two channels you have that aren't cold

You said cold. You're not entirely cold — you have two assets you built for other reasons:

**A. Veteran-owned contractor networks.** You run a veteran-and-military-focused travel agency. That gives you genuine standing in SDVOSB/VOSB communities, which are dense with construction and facilities contractors and have strong reciprocity norms. Start with: NaVOBA, your regional Veterans Business Outreach Center (VBOC), state veteran business chambers, LinkedIn veteran-contractor groups, and SDVOSB primes doing federal construction work. This converts "cold" to "warm intro" faster than anything else available to you.

**B. Holdfast Cyber's prospect list.** CMMC readiness prospects are defense contractors. A large share are facilities, construction, and industrial services firms doing DoD work — who have both credential requirements and a demonstrated willingness to buy compliance software from you. Any list you've already built for Holdfast is a FieldCred list. **Do not cross-sell in the same conversation** — but the names are reusable and the relationship is warm.

### The pilot kickoff — do all four of these or the pilot is dead

1. **Signed pilot agreement** (one page): 30-day term, **stated end date**, **stated price at conversion**, no obligation. This single document is the difference between 45% conversion and 15%. A free pilot without an end date becomes free forever.
2. **You load the roster.** They email you a spreadsheet; you run it through the CSV importer. Never make a pilot do data entry. This is the #1 pilot killer.
3. **One site configured with credential requirements**, gate QR generated, printed sign handed over.
4. **One real gate scan performed on the call**, with them watching. They need to see CLEARED on a screen inside the first 30 minutes.

## 3. The outbound motion (the thing that actually produces revenue)

### First touch — cold email

Subject lines that work on this persona (test two):
- `cert tracking at the [Site Name] gate`
- `question about your ISN roster`

Body — under 90 words, one ask, no attachments:

> [Name] — you're listed in ISNetworld as an electrical sub, so I'm guessing your guys' OSHA cards and site orientations get checked at gates fairly often.
>
> I built FieldCred because the tracking usually lives in a spreadsheet and nobody finds out a card expired until a GC's guy catches it at the gate, in front of the client.
>
> It tracks every cert, flags expirations early, and a supervisor scans a worker's QR code at the gate to see if he's cleared. No worker logins.
>
> Worth 15 minutes? I'll show you a live scan, not slides.
>
> — Dustin

**Why this works:** it names their prequal listing (proof you did homework), names the trigger moment from `audience-icp.md` verbatim, states the mechanism concretely, and asks for a demo not a "chat."

### The demo — 20 minutes, not 45

1. **(2 min)** "Tell me how you know today whether a guy's cards are current." Let them describe the spreadsheet. Do not pitch over this.
2. **(3 min)** Directory + one worker with an expiring cert. "This is what nobody sees until it's too late."
3. **(5 min)** **Scan a QR code live on your phone.** CLEARED. Then scan an expired one. NOT CLEARED. This is the whole sale. Do it early and do it real.
4. **(3 min)** Admin dashboard — everything expiring in 60 days, CSV export.
5. **(2 min)** Audit pack — per-site printable compliance report.
6. **(5 min)** The pilot ask: *"Send me your roster spreadsheet. I'll load it myself, you'll have it running by Friday. Free for 30 days, then it's $99/month locked for a year if you keep it. I'll send a one-pager to sign — it just has the end date and the price on it."*

### What to say when they ask "who else uses this?"

Don't dodge it and don't invent traction. From `messaging-positioning.md` objection #2, which is the right answer:

> "You'd be among the first. That's exactly why it's free for 30 days and why you get the founding rate locked. The core product — tracking, alerts, gate verification — works end to end today; I just showed you. What I don't have yet is your logo on a page. That's the trade."

## 5. Days 31–60 — Make pilots succeed, start converting early

**Goal at day 60:** all cohort-1 pilots actively scanning, first 1–2 conversions closed, cohort 2 (3–4 more pilots) started.

### Pilot success management (~4 hrs/week)

Weekly 15-minute check-in with each active pilot. Three questions, every time:

1. How many scans this week? *(You can see this yourself in the scan log — ask anyway, it tells you if they know.)*
2. What did it catch that you would have missed?
3. What's annoying?

**Write down their exact words.** `audience-icp.md` and `messaging-positioning.md` are both explicitly built on hypothesized quotes, flagged as "replace with actual pilot-user phrasing." This is where you replace them. Real customer language outperforms written copy every time.

### The pilot health scorecard

Score each pilot weekly. Anything red at day 14 gets an intervention call, not an email.

| Signal | Green | Red |
|---|---|---|
| Roster loaded | >80% of field workers | <50% |
| Gate scans/week | 10+ | 0–2 |
| Distinct users signed in | 2+ | 1 (just the champion) |
| Responded to last check-in | Yes | No |
| Named a catch it made | Yes | No |

A pilot with zero scans in week 2 is not a pilot. Call them, find out whether the gate sign is actually up, and if it isn't, go fix it yourself.

### Start conversion at day 45, not day 60

Two weeks before the pilot end date, on the check-in call:

> "Your pilot's up on the 12th. Based on what you've been doing with it — [specific thing: 'you caught two expired cards in week 3'] — I'd put you on Starter at $99/month, locked for 12 months. Want me to send the payment link, or is there something that needs to work better first?"

The second half of that question is the important one. If they say "something needs to work better," you have two weeks to fix it — which is exactly why you ask at day 45 and not day 60.

### Keep outbound running

Do not stop prospecting to service pilots. Drop to 20 touches/week but do not go to zero — cohort 2 is what makes day 120 non-zero, and a pipeline restarted from cold takes 6 weeks to warm back up.

## 6. Days 61–90 — Convert, prove, and get referrals

**Goal at day 90:** 3–5 paying customers, one written case study, two referral introductions in flight.

### Week 9–10: Convert cohort 1

Run the conversion conversation with every cohort-1 pilot. Expect three outcomes:

- **Yes** → payment link, FTP step, confirm login, welcome call. Track how long the whole thing takes; that's your onboarding SLA.
- **Not yet** → find the actual blocker. Offer a 30-day extension *once*, with a new signed end date. Never open-ended.
- **No** → **do the exit interview.** This is the most valuable data you will get all quarter. `messaging-positioning.md` explicitly flags "too early to know who FieldCred actually loses to." This is how you find out. Ask: what would have had to be true?

### Week 10: Build the one asset you've been missing

From your best pilot, write a **one-page case study**: what their tracking looked like before, what FieldCred caught, one quoted sentence from the champion. Get written approval to use their name; if they won't give it, use "a 60-worker electrical contractor" — still far better than nothing.

This unblocks a lot: it's your objection-handler for "who else uses this," your best LinkedIn post, and the thing that lets you raise price with confidence.

### Week 11: The referral ask

Every converted customer gets asked once, directly:

> "Who else has this problem? Specifically — who do you know running a similar-size crew who's still doing it in a spreadsheet?"

Safety managers talk to each other. `audience-icp.md` calls word of mouth "likely the highest-trust channel by far" — and it's the only channel that costs you zero hours per lead. Ask every single one.

### Week 12: Decide what's true

Sit down with the data and answer four questions honestly:

1. **Which persona actually converted?** Owner/ops, safety manager, or staffing lead. Rewrite `audience-icp.md` around the winner and delete the other two.
2. **Which trigger actually showed up?** Gate moment or audit moment. Aim all messaging at it.
3. **What was the real cost of acquisition in hours?** Total selling hours ÷ customers closed. If it's 30 hrs/customer, your growth ceiling at 10 hrs/week is 1 customer every 3 weeks — and that tells you whether the next 90 days are "do more of this" or "change the motion."
4. **Did anybody push back on price?** If nobody did, you're too cheap. Raise it for cohort 2.
