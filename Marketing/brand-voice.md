# FieldCred — Brand Voice Guide

**What this file is:** The standing voice and style reference for all FieldCred marketing — website, LinkedIn, email, sales collateral. If copy conflicts with this file, the copy is wrong.

**The brand in one line:** FieldCred keeps crews cleared to work — tracking worker certifications, flagging expirations before they bite, and letting supervisors verify anyone at the gate with a QR scan — built for the field, not the office.

**The name:** Field + credential. The point of the name is where the product lives: credentials that work at the gate, on the tailgate, in the rain — not in a filing cabinet back at the office. Let that show up in tone; don't explain the name to death in copy.

**Stage note (confirmed 2026-07-18):** FieldCred is pre-launch. The full core loop — cert tracking, expiration alerts, QR gate verification — is built and works end-to-end. Early access is free during the pilot; paid pricing is not yet set. Voice direction below confirmed by Dustin.

---

## Personality: 4 traits

**1. Field-first**
- *This means:* We talk like the jobsite, not the back office. Every feature is described by what it does at the gate, at 6 a.m., on a phone with gloves on.
- *This doesn't mean:* Playing dress-up as a hard hat. No forced tough-guy talk, no "boots on the ground" filler. Respect the trades by being useful, not by imitating them.

**2. Straight-talking**
- *This means:* We tell buyers exactly what the product does and doesn't do — including that we're new and in pilot. Plain words, real capability, no vaporware promises.
- *This doesn't mean:* Underselling. The core loop works today; say so with confidence.

**3. Reliably boring about compliance**
- *This means:* Credential tracking is unglamorous and the stakes are real — a lapsed cert is a worker sent home, a failed audit, a stop-work order. We treat it with steady, procedural seriousness. Everything has a status, an expiration date, and an owner.
- *This doesn't mean:* Dull copy. The product is boring in the best way — the marketing can still have a pulse.

**4. The friction-remover**
- *This means:* The enemy is the office callback, the binder in the truck, the login nobody remembers. We lead with what we remove: no logins for field verification, no phone calls to HR, no spreadsheet archaeology.
- *This doesn't mean:* Pretending compliance itself goes away. The requirement stays; the friction goes.

---

## Tone by context

**Website:** Confident and concrete. Short sentences. Lead with the buyer's moment ("The GC wants training records before your crew mobilizes Monday"), not with us. One idea per section.

**LinkedIn:** Practical and observational. Share what's actually happening in field compliance — expired-cert failure patterns, audit realities, useful checklists. No engagement bait, no "Agree?"

**Email:** Brief. Direct. One clear next step per email. The reader is a safety manager or ops lead reading on a phone between site visits.

**Sales collateral / demos:** Show, don't adjective. Screenshots and the QR scan itself beat paragraphs. Be plain about pilot terms: free early access, real product, feedback welcome.

## Vocabulary

**Words and phrases we use:**
cleared to work · verified at the gate · scan, see, wave through · before it expires · who's current, who's not · no logins, no callbacks · the wallet card problem · crew roster · site-ready · free early access

**Words and phrases we never use:**
- Fear-mongering: "one incident from disaster," "OSHA is coming for you," "your next fine is waiting"
- Buzzword filler: synergy, holistic, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, turnkey, leverage (as a verb), "digital transformation"
- Overpromises: "guaranteed compliance," "audit-proof," "never fail an inspection again"
- **False traction:** Nothing that implies an existing customer base, usage stats, testimonials, or integrations. There are none citable yet (confirmed 2026-07-18). No "trusted by contractors nationwide," no "join hundreds of crews," no invented pilot anecdotes.
- **Unearned status:** Never state or imply that FieldCred is endorsed by, approved by, or affiliated with OSHA, any licensing board, or any certification issuer. FieldCred tracks and verifies records; it does not issue credentials.

---

## Style rules

1. **Sentence length:** Mostly short. One breath. Vary occasionally so it doesn't read robotic.
2. **Acronyms and jargon:** Define on first use in every standalone piece — OSHA 10/30, EMR, TWIC, MSHA, whatever appears. The reader knows their trade, not necessarily every credential regime.
3. **Fear and urgency:** Urgency is allowed; fear-selling is not. The honest stake is operational — "a worker with a lapsed cert gets sent home and the crew short-hands the pour" — stated plainly, once, without doom music.
4. **Technical depth:** Lead with the operational outcome (who's cleared, who's flagged, how fast the gate check is). Put admin and data-handling detail in the follow-up for the office buyer who wants it.
5. **Claims discipline:** Describe the product and the problem — nothing else. No customers, stats, testimonials, integrations, or third-party validation of any kind until Dustin confirms something citable exists. Feature claims limited to the shipped core loop: tracking, expiration alerts, QR gate verification.
6. **Pricing:** "Free early access during the pilot" is citable. Nothing else about pricing — no future price points, no "affordable," no comparisons. Pricing isn't set.
7. **Pilot honesty:** Being new is not a secret. Frame early access as the offer it is — free, real, shaped by pilot feedback — never disguised as an established product.

## Visual identity (established 2026-07-18, Dustin's pick)

The pilot landing page (pilot-landing-industrial.html) sets the visual direction: **industrial/jobsite**. All future FieldCred visuals — site pages, social graphics, decks, one-pagers — should match it.

- **Palette:** Dark charcoal base (`#14171a` / `#1d2126` / `#262b31`), warm off-white text (`#f4f2ed`), **safety yellow** accent (`#f5b400`). Status colors: green `#3fb950` (current/cleared), yellow (renewal window), red `#e5534b` (expired/error).
- **Type:** Barlow Condensed (bold, uppercase) for headlines; Barlow for body. System-font fallbacks acceptable.
- **Motifs:** Hazard-stripe rules (yellow/charcoal diagonal) as top/bottom borders; left-edge yellow bars on feature cards; status badges styled like gate-check results.
- **Rule of restraint:** The look is jobsite, not costume — no clip-art hard hats, no distressed textures, no camo. The hazard stripe and yellow do the work.

## Before / after examples

**Example 1 — killing the fear-sell**
- ❌ *Before:* "One expired cert could mean crippling OSHA fines, lawsuits, and a shutdown site. Can you afford the risk?"
- ✅ *After:* "If a worker's card lapsed last month and nobody caught it, he's not cleared to work — and you find out at the worst possible time. FieldCred flags it weeks ahead."

**Example 2 — killing the buzzwords**
- ❌ *Before:* "FieldCred is a best-in-class workforce compliance platform leveraging cutting-edge verification technology."
- ✅ *After:* "FieldCred tracks every worker's certs, warns you before they expire, and lets a supervisor verify anyone at the gate with a phone scan. That's the whole pitch."

**Example 3 — killing false traction**
- ❌ *Before:* "Join the hundreds of contractors who trust FieldCred to keep their crews compliant."
- ✅ *After:* "FieldCred is new, it works, and early access is free while we're in pilot. Bring a real crew roster and see if it holds up."

**Example 4 — killing the jargon wall**
- ❌ *Before:* "Our platform enables real-time credential lifecycle management with QR-based attestation workflows for distributed workforces."
- ✅ *After:* "Every worker gets a QR code. The gate supervisor scans it and sees, in two seconds, whether that worker's OSHA card, license, and site orientation are current. No login. No call to the office."
