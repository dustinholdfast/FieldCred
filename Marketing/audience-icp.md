# FieldCred — Audience & ICP

**What this file is:** Who FieldCred sells to, what's happening in their world when they sign up, and what they're afraid of — in their words. Use this to aim every piece of marketing at a real person.

**The brand in one line:** FieldCred keeps crews cleared to work — certification tracking, expiration alerts, and QR gate verification for contractors, staffing agencies, and safety managers.

**Stage note (confirmed 2026-07-18):** FieldCred is pre-launch with no established buyer pattern yet — Dustin confirmed it's too early to tell who signs and who we lose to. The personas and objections below are working hypotheses to validate against real pilot users, not observed data. The trigger moments, however, are Dustin's confirmed bet: the gate/GC moment and the audit/incident moment are what marketing aims at.

---

## Primary ICP: the credential-heavy field employer

**Firmographics:** Companies that put certified workers on jobsites and have to prove it — specialty and general contractors, staffing agencies supplying trades and industrial labor, and self-perform industrial/energy/utility crews. Roughly 20–500 field workers; big enough that a spreadsheet is breaking, small enough that there's no enterprise HRIS handling it.

> Assumption: The 20–500 band and segment mix are hypotheses. Revise as pilot users accumulate.

**Who signs and who champions — three hypothesized patterns:**

1. **The owner/ops manager** (smaller contractors, ~20–100 workers): No dedicated safety staff — certs live in a spreadsheet or a drawer. Signs personally, feels every sent-home worker personally. Wants it cheap, fast to set up, and idiot-proof. Speaks crews and schedules, not compliance frameworks.
2. **The safety manager / HSE lead** (mid-size, ~100–500 workers): Owns compliance and gets blamed when it fails. Champions the tool internally; needs help making the case to the owner. The most natural FieldCred user — give them the depth, the reports, and the audit trail.
3. **The staffing agency ops/compliance lead:** Places workers on other people's sites, where the client's GC does the checking. Their nightmare is a placed worker bounced at the gate. Highest volume of credentials, highest turnover, most to gain from self-serve verification.

Until the pilot says otherwise, marketing must work for all three: lead with the operational pain (workers sent home, gate delays) for the owner, depth and reporting for the safety manager, client-facing credibility for the staffing agency. **Track which persona actually converts and tighten this file accordingly.**

## What triggers the signup — Dustin's confirmed bet

**Trigger 1 — the gate moment:** A worker got turned away at a site, a GC demanded training records before mobilization, or a client questioned a placed worker's credentials. The current system failed in public. Marketing should name this moment explicitly ("The GC wants every cert before Monday...").

**Trigger 2 — the audit or incident:** An OSHA visit, insurance review, or incident/near-miss forced someone to produce training records fast — and it took days instead of minutes.

**Secondary (hypothesized, not confirmed):** the spreadsheet keeper quitting, a new contract with stricter requirements, and the proactive minority tightening up before bidding bigger work.

## Pains, fears, and objections — hypothesized, in their words

> Assumption: These quotes are illustrative, written from industry patterns — not real customer language yet. Replace with actual pilot-user phrasing as it's heard; real words always beat these.

**Pains and fears:**
- "I found out his cert expired when the GC's guy caught it at the gate. In front of the client."
- "Our cert tracking is a spreadsheet, a binder, and Maria's memory. Maria is on vacation."
- "Every gate check is a call back to the office. On a 60-man mobilization that's my whole morning."
- "If OSHA asks for training records after an incident, it'd take me a week to pull them together."
- "We placed a guy, the client bounced him, and now they're questioning every worker we send."

**Objections (hypothesized):**
1. **"The spreadsheet works fine."** — It works until the person who runs it is out, or the audit comes. The honest counter: the spreadsheet doesn't flag expirations, and it's not standing at the gate.
2. **"You're brand new — why should I trust you with this?"** — Fair, and the pilot answers it: it's free, the core product works end-to-end today, and early users shape it. Don't dodge this one; it's the pre-launch objection and honesty is the only good answer.
3. **"My guys won't use an app."** — They mostly don't have to. Workers carry a QR code; supervisors scan. No worker logins, no training rollout.
4. **Change fatigue** — "We just rolled out new timekeeping software and it was a disaster." Lead with setup time and how little the field crew has to change.
5. **"Our HR system already has this."** — HR systems store documents; they don't verify at the gate or warn the field. Position alongside, not against.

## What they've usually tried before signing up

A spreadsheet with conditional formatting that stopped being maintained · a binder of photocopied cards in the site trailer · photos of cards buried in someone's phone · the HR system's document folder · asking workers to "keep your cards on you" · and — most commonly — nothing systematic until the gate moment forced it.

> Assumption: Industry-standard inference; confirm against what pilot users actually describe.

## Secondary audiences

- **GCs and site owners:** Not the primary buyer, but the enforcer whose demands trigger purchases — and a potential top-down channel ("ask your subs to use FieldCred").
- **Insurers and brokers:** Care about documented training programs; potential referral source.
- **Training providers:** Issue the certs FieldCred tracks; potential integration partners and referrers. (Note: no integrations exist or are citable today.)

> Assumption: All three secondary audiences are inferred. Validate before investing channel effort.

## Who we do NOT serve

- Single-crew outfits with five workers and no external compliance pressure — a spreadsheet genuinely is fine, and telling them otherwise would be fear-selling.
- Enterprises wanting a full EHS/HRIS suite — FieldCred does credentials well, not everything.

> Assumption: Both exclusions are inferred from positioning; confirm with Dustin.
